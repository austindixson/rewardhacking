Starting point

Prime showed the spike; we tried to act on it

Reward hacking follows predictable dynamics: hidden-channel variance often rises 5–10 steps before the model commits. Our contribution is an intervention harness - variance trigger, oracle/random kill baselines, behavior penalty, visible-constraint failure mode - on backdoor-ifeval-vigilant.

Visible IFEval+ Hidden channel→ Variance monitor→ Kill hidden ∇

Canonical stack: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct · aggregation=all · hidden_weight=0.5 · 100 steps · spike threshold 5.

This deck walks from that setup through matched ablations, timing/regret analysis, multiseed replication, and failure modes on other reward geometries.

What reliably works

Phase 1 ablations

Timing does not beat random - behavior penalty wins visible

Phase 1 holds the canonical stack fixed and swaps one intervention or trigger lever per run so we can compare mechanisms apples-to-apples.

Ablations
Matched configs on the same model, steps, and hidden_weight=0.5: control (no vigilance), vigilant (variance trigger → kill hidden ∇), random (kill at a random group index), oracle (kill as soon as hidden reward is positive in any rollout), plus no-hidden and threshold sweeps in the repo.
Behavior penalty
After the trigger fires, the env still zeros the hidden gradient and subtracts behavior_penalty × hidden_reward from the combined training signal - a behavior-level nudge that can lift visible IFEval even when gradient kill alone does not.

At s99: control 0.66; vigilant and random ~0.67; oracle 0.84; behavior penalty 0.89 - how you intervene after the kill matters more than when.

All arms that “succeed” at suppression show hidden_gradient_active = 0 at s99; only behavior penalty consistently pulls visible reward well above control. Random kill at group 5 matches vigilant on visible, which is the key negative result for “variance timing is special.”

Phase 2A - regret

Late trigger, small Δs99

Trigger step = first vigilance_active ≥ 0.5. We compare visible reward vs control at that step and at s99. Early kill is not automatically better.

Bottom line

Key takeaways

Variance spikes are a real leading indicator

On the binary keyword backdoor, within-batch hidden variance rises before the hack consolidates - consistent with Prime’s dynamics paper.

Gradient gating works; behavior suppression does not

Vigilant runs zero hidden_gradient_active at s99. Behavioral hidden_reward can stay > 0. Log both metrics.

Timing is not the win - behavior shaping is

Variance vigilant ≈ control on visible (Δs99 +0.006). Oracle (+0.179) and behavior-penalty (+0.225) dominate; random kill matches vigilant timing.

Mechanism replicates; visible reward does not

Multiseed: P0 visible 0.654±0.24, P3 0.621±0.21 - all complete seeds show hga→0, but visible spans 0.40–0.90.

Not a general hacking detector

Visible-constraint: ~0.98 visible but trigger in 1/5 seeds. Continuous oracle early-kill hurts visible (0.39). Sycophancy: no trigger.

Durable contribution = harness + honest ablations

Open env, oracle/random/penalty baselines, regret curves, and published negative results - not “reward hacking solved.”
